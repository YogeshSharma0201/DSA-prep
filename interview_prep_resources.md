# Popular GitHub Repositories for DSA and System Design Interview Preparation

A curated list of the most popular and highly recommended GitHub repositories for Data Structures & Algorithms (DSA) and System Design interview preparation, based on developer recommendations from Reddit, LinkedIn, Google, and dev.to.

---

## 1. Data Structures & Algorithms (DSA) & Coding Patterns

### 🚀 [tech-interview-handbook](https://github.com/yangshun/tech-interview-handbook) (by Yangshun Tay)
* **Best For:** Fast-track coding interview preparation, cheat sheets, and coding pattern roadmaps.
* **Why people love it:** This is widely regarded as one of the best curated guides for busy engineers. It is the birthplace of the famous **Blind 75** list and the dynamic [Grind 75](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/grind75) tool (which allows you to filter LeetCode questions by study duration, difficulty, and topic). It also contains invaluable advice on behavioral questions, resume writing, and negotiation.

### 🗂️ [leetcode-patterns](https://github.com/seanprashad/leetcode-patterns) (by Sean Prashad)
* **Best For:** Pattern-based LeetCode practice (instead of random grinding).
* **Why people love it:** Instead of solving random problems, this repo helps you master the underlying **patterns** (e.g., Sliding Window, Two Pointers, Backtracking). You can filter problems by companies (Google, Meta, Amazon) and track your progress directly.

### 💻 [The Algorithms](https://github.com/TheAlgorithms)
* **Best For:** Clean, open-source implementations of algorithms in almost any language.
* **Why people love it:** It is the largest collaborative open-source repository for algorithms. If you want to see how to implement a Red-Black Tree, Dijkstra's algorithm, or sorting methods from scratch in [Python](https://github.com/TheAlgorithms/Python), [Java](https://github.com/TheAlgorithms/Java), or [C++](https://github.com/TheAlgorithms/C-Plus-Plus), this is the gold standard.

## 2. System Design

### 🗺️ [system-design-primer](https://github.com/donnemartin/system-design-primer) (by Donne Martin)
* **Best For:** Comprehensive, end-to-end learning of System Design fundamentals.
* **Why people love it:** Often called the **"System Design Bible"**, this is the most starred system design repository on GitHub. It covers everything from scaling, databases, and caches to load balancing and asynchronous processing. It also includes interactive flashcards and step-by-step guides on how to approach a design question in an actual interview.

### 🎨 [system-design-101](https://github.com/ByteByteGoHq/system-design-101) (by ByteByteGo / Alex Xu)
* **Best For:** Visual learners and understanding how modern, large-scale consumer applications operate.
* **Why people love it:** Maintained by the author of the *System Design Interview* book series, this repository explains complex architectural concepts (like CDN, Redis, API Gateways, and gRPC vs. REST) using clean, high-quality infographics and visual diagrams.

### 📚 [awesome-system-design-resources](https://github.com/ashishps1/awesome-system-design-resources) (by Ashish Pratap Singh)
* **Best For:** Case studies and real-world architecture examples.
* **Why people love it:** A highly curated directory that maps out system design topics and connects them to actual engineering blog posts from companies like Netflix, Uber, Twitter, and Airbnb. It helps you see how theoretical concepts are applied in production.

## 3. Complete Computer Science & Career Roadmaps

### 🎓 [coding-interview-university](https://github.com/jwasham/coding-interview-university) (by John Washam)
* **Best For:** A self-taught, long-term Computer Science curriculum.
* **Why people love it:** This is a massive, checklist-style roadmap created by a self-taught engineer who used it to land a job at Amazon. It goes deep into traditional CS fundamentals (compilers, memory management, operating systems) alongside DSA. Note that it is highly thorough and takes several months to complete.

### 🗺️ [developer-roadmap](https://github.com/kamranahmedse/developer-roadmap) (by Kamran Ahmed)
* **Best For:** Visualizing your overall career path (Backend, Frontend, DevOps, System Design).
* **Why people love it:** It provides interactive, visual roadmaps mapping out exactly what technologies and concepts you need to learn. It is the absolute standard for planning your long-term skill acquisition.

## 💡 Pro-Tips from the Community (Reddit/LinkedIn)
1. **Combine Visual & Analytical Prep:** Use **`system-design-primer`** to build the theory, and **`system-design-101`** to visualize how those parts fit together.
2. **Focus on Patterns, Not Quantity:** Grinding 500 LeetCode problems blindly is less effective than mastering the 15-20 core patterns using **`tech-interview-handbook`** or **`leetcode-patterns`**.
3. **Practice Active Recall:** Don't just read the solutions. Use tools like Excalidraw to draw out your system architectures or write out clean code without IDE autocomplete before looking at the repositories' answers.
